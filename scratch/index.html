Scratch: Game Development & Storytelling

From the Scratch interface to a finished project presented to the class. Each week teaches one idea and gives students something playable or viewable to build.

Key philosophy

Learn by doing. Each week introduces a concept, then students immediately practice by building something playable or viewable.

Target students
Ages 7–11, all with laptops.
Starting point
Students already know basic Scratch ideas (what sprites are, the interface). This course goes deeper.
End goal
Every student builds and presents one complete Scratch game or interactive story.
Focus areas
Equal emphasis on game development and storytelling / animation.

Teaching tips for all 10 weeks

🎬

Start with a demo

Open every class with a cool 30-second Scratch video (1–2 min). It gets kids excited.

👩‍🏫

Code together first

Every lesson, build the main example as a class, step by step. Then let them customise.

🧪

Let them explore

After explaining a concept, give 10 minutes of free play. Let them try wild ideas.

🐞

Celebrate bugs

When something breaks, celebrate! "Great! Now we get to debug." Debugging teaches problem-solving.

🤝

Pair & share

Sit in pairs: one codes, one watches. Switch halfway. Builds collaboration.

🌐

Use Scratch Studio

Create a class studio online so students can share projects and comment on each other's work.

🖼️

Keep a project gallery

Print or screenshot finished projects and hang them up. Pride + motivation.

🎚️

Differentiate

Advanced students add leaderboards, levels or AI enemies. Struggling students do one feature well.

Suggested project ideas by interest

🎮 Game-focused

  • Dodge game: avoid falling obstacles
  • Collectible game: catch or grab items
  • Racing game: cross the finish line first
  • Pong clone: hit the ball back and forth
  • Shooting game: click or press space to hit targets

📖 Storytelling-focused

  • Animated adventure: a character explores a magical world
  • Dialogue-driven story: characters talk, the player chooses replies
  • Educational story: teach a concept through animation
  • Comic-style narrative: a series of scenes with captions
  • Music video: animate sprites to music

✨ Both game & story

  • Interactive mystery: solve clues to advance the story
  • Quest game: collect items in sequence to unlock the next scene
  • Fable or fairy tale: tell a classic story with game elements
  • Recipe or tutorial: teach someone how to do something, game-style

Assessment

Week 10 showcase rubric

  • ☑ Project runs without major bugs
  • ☑ Student can explain what the code does
  • ☑ At least 2–3 concepts from weeks 1–9 are used
  • ☑ The project is creative or fun (not just a copy)
  • ☑ Student presented clearly

Throughout the course

  • Check that students can explain concepts in their own words.
  • Look at their projects each week to see understanding.
  • Ask: "What would happen if you changed this block?" — it shows thinking.
  • Listen to peer feedback sessions.

By Week 10, students will have…

  • Built and run working programs in Scratch
  • Learned programming fundamentals (events, loops, conditionals, variables)
  • Collaborated with peers
  • Created and presented a complete game or interactive story
  • Built confidence in learning new technology

Next steps after Week 10

  • Extend to 2-player or networked projects
  • Explore advanced features: cloning, lists, more complex logic
  • Transition to Python or JavaScript — they already know programming!
  • Make physical projects: robots or Arduino boards
  • Create games for a cause: educational games or awareness campaigns
